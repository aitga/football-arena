Football Formations Explained: 4-4-2, 4-3-3 and 3-5-2

By Football Arena · 04 November 2026

When commentators say a team is playing "4-3-3", they are describing how the ten outfield players are arranged. The numbers always go from the back to the front, and the goalkeeper is never counted.

4-4-2: the classic

Four defenders, four midfielders and two strikers. It is simple, balanced and easy for players to understand. Each player has a clear partner, and the two strikers can help each other up front. Its weakness is that the middle of the pitch can be outnumbered by teams with three central midfielders.

4-3-3: control and width

Four defenders, three central midfielders and three attackers, usually one central striker and two wide forwards. This shape is popular with teams that like to keep the ball, because the three midfielders form a triangle that makes passing easier. The wide forwards stretch the defence and often cut inside to shoot.

3-5-2: wing-backs everywhere

Three centre-backs, five midfielders and two strikers. The two widest midfielders are called wing-backs: they defend like full-backs and attack like wingers, which makes it one of the most tiring roles in football. The extra centre-back gives security, and the busy midfield helps win the ball back.

4-2-3-1: the modern favourite

Two holding midfielders protect the back four, while three attacking midfielders support a lone striker. The player in the middle of the three, the "number 10", is usually the most creative player on the pitch.

Formations change during the match

A formation is only a starting point. A team might defend in a 4-4-2 and then attack in a 2-3-5 as full-backs push forward. Coaches now talk about shapes "with the ball" and "without the ball" instead of one fixed formation.

Which one is best?

There is no perfect formation. The best one is the shape that fits the players a coach has. A team with fast wingers might love 4-3-3, while a team with strong defenders might prefer three centre-backs.
